References for Learning to optimize a serving system, by Pritish Saha. Updated 22 November 2026.

This page collects the background papers, exact code revisions and public reports relevant to the project. The article's measurements come from the saved project results.

The course and paper list includes suggested reading beyond what was studied during the competition. For implementation reports, the tables note which parts the research actually covered. Other authors' benchmarks were not independently reproduced here.

Papers behind the system

These papers explain the methods used by the system. For the exact behavior of the competition build, follow the pinned implementation links in the next section.

  1. Gene M. Amdahl (1967). Validity of the single processor approach to achieving large scale computing capabilities. AFIPS Spring Joint Computer Conference, pp. 483–485. The article uses the familiar fixed-work speedup model to distinguish a component gain from a system gain; it is an illustrative calculation, not a fitted model of these runs.
  2. Lianmin Zheng et al. (2024). SGLang: Efficient Execution of Structured Language Model Programs. Background for the serving runtime and cache reuse. The paper predates the pinned Muse implementation and does not specify its current DFlash, FA3 or Marlin paths.
  3. Elias Frantar, Roberto L. Castro, Jiale Chen, Torsten Hoefler and Dan Alistarh (2024). MARLIN: Mixed-Precision Auto-Regressive Parallel Inference on Large Language Models. Background for efficient weight-quantized inference. Original implementation. The experiment uses SGLang's later NVFP4 MoE adaptation, not the paper's original kernel unchanged.
  4. Jay Shah, Ganesh Bikshandi, Ying Zhang, Vijay Thakkar, Pradeep Ramani and Tri Dao (2024). FlashAttention-3: Fast and Accurate Attention with Asynchrony and Low-precision. Credit for the downstream attention algorithm. The final patch changes input preparation, not the FA3 attention algorithm. The paper's reported speedups are not this project's results.
  5. Jian Chen, Yesheng Liang and Zhijian Liu (2026). DFlash: Block Diffusion for Flash Speculative Decoding. ICML proceedings. Background for parallel drafting and target verification. The actual competition used the Muse-specific DFlash2 model and its fixed configuration; the original paper is not a substitute for that model card or worker code.
  6. Biao Zhang and Rico Sennrich (2019). Root Mean Square Layer Normalization. Credit for RMS normalization. The article's reduction-order bug was diagnosed from the exact kernel and saved failures, not derived from the paper.
  7. Jianlin Su et al. (2023 revision of a 2021 preprint). RoFormer: Enhanced Transformer with Rotary Position Embedding. Credit for RoPE. The pinned Muse implementation determines its interleaving, lookup tables and rounding sequence.

The PyTorch FP8 conversion claim is version-specific: the independent A review inspected installed PyTorch 2.14.1, torch/headeronly/util/Float8_e4m3fn.h, fp8e4m3fn_from_fp32_value, and the project tested actual conversions. Its header hash and correction are preserved in the archived A review. A generic current PyTorch documentation page would not establish those exact bytes. That historical review's “no blocker” conclusion was subsequently superseded by the boundary failures and repair; only its conversion-source evidence is cited here.

The longer preparation catalog also listed vLLM, Orca, Sarathi-Serve, NanoFlow, DistServe, GPU MODE, Harvard/MIT courses, MLC and other resources. Being present in a catalog is not evidence of use. They are not presented as sources of this implementation or as completed study in this article.

Project evidence & citation scope

  • Claim-to-artifact map: which saved result or probe supports each project claim.
  • Source inventory: original project paths, sizes and full SHA256 hashes, including the initial reading register, later addendum and implementation reviews.
  • Best completed evaluation and final repaired evaluation: the source of the article's reported serving ratios.
  • Repair source/local review, independent dense-sweep addendum, and repair delta: final numerical-repair evidence.
  • Initial mapping patch: the first global-to-local GPU route lookup.
  • Final-selection extract and submission technical note: what was frozen, what remained queued and which risks were recorded before the scored run. These are historical decision records, not current status.
  • Chart data: the plotted values, with no invented uncertainty estimates.

All conceptual diagrams are original; editable Excalidraw scenes accompany them. No public benchmark table is relabeled as this project's result. Public reports are credited for the ideas they supplied, while unsuccessful, unimplemented and unread branches remain identified as such.
